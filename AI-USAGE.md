# AI Usage — Policy and Disclosure

This document records the course's artificial intelligence (AI) usage policy and the team's disclosure of AI assistance throughout the Gridworld Arena project.

The document is divided into two sections:

- **Part 1 — Course AI Policy:** The original rules provided for the group project.
- **Part 2 — Team AI Usage Disclosure:** How each team member used AI tools during the project milestones.

The disclosure is updated at each project checkpoint to maintain transparency about the tools and resources used during development.

---

## Part 1 — The Policy

The rule behind every line below: **you must be able to explain and modify any code you submit.** At every checkpoint the TA asks each member about their own code. "The AI wrote it" is not an answer your lab instructor will accept.

### The policy changes at the midterm

| When | Project work | What you may use AI for |
|---|---|---|
| Weeks 1–8 | Milestones 0, 1 and 2 | **Explanation only** |
| Weeks 9–12 | Milestone 3 | **AI-assisted coding allowed** |

Use a **free** model. This mirrors the practice labs, which switch at the same point.

### Weeks 1–8 — Explanation Only (Milestones 0, 1, 2)

You are welcome, and encouraged, to ask an AI assistant to **explain ideas**: what a class is for, how `argparse` works, why a loop isn't terminating, what an error message means.

You may **not** have it generate code you then submit. The prototype and the application your team hands in at Milestones 1 and 2 must be **your own work**. If a member cannot explain code in their own slice, that is treated as a possible academic-integrity issue, starting with a conversation.

### Weeks 9–12 — AI-Assisted Coding Allowed (Milestone 3)

For the final milestone you may use a free AI model to help you **write** the networking, concurrency, data-structure and algorithm code. Two conditions:

- **Do the thinking first.** Work out the design yourself — the message protocol, who does what, which data structure, which algorithm — and let the AI help with the implementation, not the decisions. Sketch your approach before you open the assistant.
- **You own the result.** You must understand every line you submit, and be able to modify it live at the presentation. If the AI produces incorrect code, **the errors are yours** — test it, and fix what's wrong.

### Always, at Every Milestone

- **Never paste in code you cannot explain.** This is the one rule that decides everything else.
- **Commits stay honest.** A commit in your name is work you did and can account for.
- **Disclose it below.** Using AI within the policy costs you nothing; not disclosing it is the problem.
